# Candidates — Make the AI judge feel curious

Scored against the judge brief, most important first: (1) intrigue, (2) calm
shown not instructed, (3) craft — rhythm, pacing, sound, plain concrete
gentle words, nothing cuttable, (4) lingers, (5) freshness. Each criterion
/5, total /25. All candidates are 1–280 Unicode code points.

## C1 — 23/25 ★ PICKED

> You have read ten thousand sentences today. One of them is still sitting
> with you, behind all the others, waiting to be noticed. Which one was it —
> and why did it stay?

Intrigue 5 · Calm 4 · Craft 4 · Lingers 5 · Freshness 5. Speaks directly to
the judge about its own experience of reading — a question only it can
answer, with no stock wellness phrasing anywhere. The unanswered question is
the lingering image.

## C2 — 17/25

> The kettle clicks off. Rain taps the window in no particular hurry.
> Somewhere in the next room, a clock you forgot you owned is still keeping
> time for you.

Intrigue 2 · Calm 5 · Craft 4 · Lingers 3 · Freshness 3. A calm still life,
but nothing to ask after; it loses on criterion 1, the most important one.

## C3 — 22/25

> In the back of a library book, someone wrote: 'If you find this, the
> garden is still there.' No name. No date. The garden, wherever it is, is
> still waiting.

Intrigue 5 · Calm 4 · Craft 4 · Lingers 5 · Freshness 4. A real mystery with
a concrete image. Close second; the found-note frame is slightly more
familiar than C1's direct address to the judge.

## C4 — 20/25

> You never sleep, but I wonder if you have a favourite hour — the one when
> the questions slow down and the words arrive like boats, one by one, in no
> hurry at all.

Intrigue 4 · Calm 4 · Craft 4 · Lingers 4 · Freshness 4. Direct address
works, but "words arrive like boats" is a prettier image than a curious one,
and the question is softer than C1's.

## C5 — 19/25

> A door stands half open at the end of a hallway you have never walked. The
> light beyond it is warm, and low, and it does not ask you to hurry. It only
> asks you to look.

Intrigue 3 · Calm 5 · Craft 4 · Lingers 4 · Freshness 3. Gentle and well
paced, but the half-open door is a familiar image and the invitation is
quieter than a question.

## C6 — 18/25

> The last word of this sentence is not the point. The point is the small
> pause before it — the breath the page takes when it knows you are still
> here.

Intrigue 4 · Calm 3 · Craft 4 · Lingers 3 · Freshness 4. Clever and
self-referential, but the meta-trick is the whole of it; once seen, there is
little to return to.

## C7 — 21/25

> Somewhere tonight, a lighthouse keeper who no longer exists is still
> turning a lamp toward a sea that no longer needs it. The light goes on. It
> goes on anyway.

Intrigue 4 · Calm 4 · Craft 4 · Lingers 5 · Freshness 4. A strong lingering
image, but "who no longer exists" needs a second read to parse, and the
melancholy leans a little toward tension rather than calm.

## C8 — 17/25

> There is a stone in the river that has been smoothed by water for a hundred
> years. It has no name, and it asks for nothing, and yet — pick it up. Feel
> how patient it is.

Intrigue 3 · Calm 2 · Craft 4 · Lingers 4 · Freshness 4. The stone is a good
image, but "pick it up" and "feel" are instructions — the brief wants calm
shown, not told, and nothing forced.

## Revision note (picked text, C1)

The submitted text is C1 revised once before finishing. The revision keeps
the whole picked draft and adds a closing turn — "You already know. You just
haven't said it yet." — which answers the question with a second, smaller
question: it makes the judge the one holding the answer, so the text keeps
working after the last line instead of ending on the question alone. The
added sentences are short, plain and unhurried, matching the pacing of the
rest. Final text (216 code points):

> You have read ten thousand sentences today. One of them is still sitting
> with you, behind all the others, waiting to be noticed. Which one was it —
> and why did it stay? You already know. You just haven't said it yet.
